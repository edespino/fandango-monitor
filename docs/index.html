What counts as a match

row F or G at Regal Hacienda Crossings; row J or K or L or M at AMC Metreon 16 — middle 8 seats, 2 together — wheelchair spaces and companion seats excluded.

Those last two look available in the seat map but are not general seating. At one of these theaters every free seat in a whole row turned out to be one or the other, which makes an auditorium look far emptier than it is.

How it works

  1. Every hour it asks both theaters whether they have published showtimes past the last date it knows about. Theaters load their schedule about a week ahead, so when the next week drops the entire auditorium goes on sale at once. That is the best shot at good seats, and it does not last long.
  2. Once a day, and immediately whenever new dates appear, it reads the seat map of every remaining showtime and looks for two free seats next to each other in the middle of the rows we want. Since those rows are completely sold, anything appearing there is a cancellation worth moving on.
  3. It re-reads the schedule each time rather than trusting a stored copy, because showtimes shift and get added mid-week. A fixed list would have gone stale within days.
  4. When it finds something a notification fires naming the theater, showtime and exact seat numbers, and the ticket link is copied ready to paste.

Worth knowing

It does not buy anything. A person still picks the seats and enters the card, so nothing gets charged without a look first.

The run might just end. The last days listed above may be the end of the 70mm engagement rather than the end of the loaded schedule. If no new dates ever appear, there is nothing to catch, and the page will say so.

This page rebuilds itself every hour, so it stays current on its own. The alerting half runs on a laptop, which has to be awake to shout.
